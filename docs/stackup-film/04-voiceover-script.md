### How to read this
- **On screen** is what the viewer is seeing at that moment, taken from the actual scene code.
- **Narration** is what she says. `/` marks a natural breath or pause. **Bold** marks gentle emphasis, not a punch.
- Times are estimates at an unhurried storytelling pace (~150 words/min plus pauses). Once the real voice exists, the timing check re-runs on the measured clips.

### The arc
*A human moment* → *the essentials, then what's left* → *my own question* → *what I explored and heard* → *the insight* → *the bigger picture* → *the gap* → *Stack Up* → *what the user experiences, step by step* → *confidence, built slowly*.

The narrator is the designer (you), speaking in first person from Act 2. In Acts 4–6 she speaks to "you", the user.

## ACT 1 — The problem

### S01 · Payday — 0:00
**On screen:** the ground line draws in. The character works at a desk, a phone buzzes, and *Salary Credited ₹35,000* rises and settles into a stack of coins.
> Your **first** salary. / It feels like freedom.

*Why:* the relatable human moment, light and proud. The pause lets the coins land.

### S02 · Essentials first, then what's left — 0:04
**On screen:** Rent, Electricity, Groceries and Transport drop in one by one, each taking a coin into its own stack. They're ticked as *Essentials covered*. The remaining coins light up as **What's left**. Heading: *Essentials covered. Now, what's left.*
> **First**, the essentials: / rent, electricity, groceries, travel. / What's left is **yours** to plan.

*Why:* matches the visual beat for beat. The list is spoken as the chips drop, and "what's left" lands as the coins light up. This is the product's founding idea: Stack Up starts with the money after essentials, not the whole salary.

### S03 · The questions — 0:11
**On screen:** the character, hand on chin, with "What's left" at their feet. Bubbles: *What do I do with what's left? · How do I track everything? · Should I save, invest or get insurance first?* They condense into one orange "?".
> That's where it gets **harder**. / Where do you even **begin**?

*Why:* the bubbles carry the specific questions, so the voice states the feeling rather than repeating them.

## ACT 2 — My journey

### S04 · These were my questions too — 0:16
**On screen:** a cursor types *You earn money. But how do you manage it?* The character, now with a notebook (you), and the live-brief card: *"Design a Personal Finance Management experience…"*, with "manage" underlined and the note *…but did I know how?*, then *I realised I first needed to understand the financial ecosystem myself.*
> I know that question well. / It was **mine**. / Starting my semester project, I realised I didn't understand money **myself**.

*Why:* the emotional turn. The story becomes personal, matching the hand-written "…but did I know how?"

### S05 · Exploring the ecosystem — 0:25
**On screen:** Investments · Payments · Credit Cards · Net Worth · Paper Trading; real screens from Groww, Kuvera, IND Money, Money Bhai and Frontpage; sticky notes: *Built for investment-ready users · Learning sits outside the journey · Practice removes the risk, not the complexity.*
> So I explored the apps people use. / Most assumed you **already** knew how to invest.

*Why:* states your first benchmark insight. The sticky notes carry the other two.

### S06 · Listening — 0:31.5
**On screen:** a light **40+** disc (*broader research, across different levels of financial experience*), then a green **23** disc (*focused conversations, early-career professionals aged 21–26*). Real quotes rise from the 23.
> Then I **listened**. / Over forty people, / and twenty-three deeper conversations with early-career professionals, / aged twenty-one to twenty-six.

*Why:* the two groups are spoken separately, exactly as they appear. The pause after "forty people" keeps them distinct.

### S07 · Four patterns, one need — 0:39.5 (now 10.5 s, up from 6)
**On screen:** *People weren't struggling in the same way.* Then each behaviour gets its own beat: *Depend on others → Figure it out themselves → Manage it in fragments → Hesitate to ask*, converging on **guidance before action.**
> Some relied on others. / Some figured it out alone. / Some managed in fragments. / Some never asked. / All needed **guidance** — before **action**.

*Why:* each sentence is spoken while its illustration is in focus. This is the heart of your research, so it gets the most time of any research scene.

*Why:* "a little more confidence" lands on the 0% → 10% moment, the most memorable product beat.

## ACT 6 — The destination

### S16 · The Confidence Jar — 1:36.5
**On screen:** *Your Confidence Jar*; the jar lifts out, and the decisions the prototype contains drop in (Practice Wallet · Mutual Funds · Guided questions · Compared alternatives · First investment). The meter rises and holds at about 80%. On screen: *Confidence, before real money.*
> Every decision fills your **Confidence Jar**, / until you're ready for real money.

*Why:* confidence grows through practice. The jar stops short of full, so the film doesn't claim a completed journey.

### S17 · Understand → Practise → Build Confidence — 1:42
**On screen:** the "What's left" coins from Act 1 return and become your steps illustration: the same character climbing *Understand → Practise → Build Confidence* towards *A More Confident You*. Then *Understand money. Build confidence. Make informed decisions.* and the end card.
> Confidence isn't **given**. / It's built — / one step at a time.

*Why:* ends on a thought rather than a sales line, echoing the character's own "One step at a time!" bubble. The closing tagline then plays **without voice**, over music only, so it reads like a title card. *If you'd rather the narrator also speak the tagline, it fits by trimming this line to "One step at a time."*

## Voice and audio direction (for generation)

- **Voice:** young adult female, Indian English. Warm, articulate, conversational, confident. A thoughtful storyteller, not an advert read, not dramatic.
- **Pace:** unhurried (~150 words/min). Real pauses at every `/`. Act 2 a touch slower and more reflective; Act 5 slightly brighter.
- **Emphasis:** lift the **bold** words gently; never punch them.
- **Music:** the existing bed stays. It ducks under the voice (to roughly a third of its level) whenever she speaks, and returns to its full level in the pauses. This is already built and switches on as soon as voice clips exist.
