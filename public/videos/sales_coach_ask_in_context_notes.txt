AUDIO CLEANUP
-------------
1. Duplicate/overlapping AI voice
   Diagnosis: the recording holds the clean system-audio voice plus a second, delayed copy
   of the same voice (~110 ms later). That's the laptop speaker being picked up again by
   the microphone. The copy came and went (5.7–6.6 s, 8.3–9.5 s, 18.4–21.9 s source time),
   and its delay drifted by microseconds (two audio clocks), so a fixed echo filter
   could not catch it.
   Fix: a drift-tracking echo canceller, run in 5 frequency bands over 0.2 s windows.
   Each window measures the copy's exact delay and level and subtracts only that copy.
   It acts only where the copy is statistically significant, so the clean parts of the
   voice are left essentially untouched (changes there are 20–38 dB down, i.e. inaudible).
   Result: copy-correlation in the echo zones went from 0.33 / 0.26 / 0.16 / 0.22
   to 0.11 / 0.11 / 0.07 / 0.05 (≈0.05 is the normal level for single-voice speech).
2. Background noise
   Spectral noise reduction, profiled only from voice-free stretches (before the answer,
   between sentences, after the answer).
   - Mic hiss before the AI speaks: -23.8 dB -> -58.2 dB (effectively silent)
   - Noise floor between sentences: -29.2 dB -> -40.7 dB
   - 70 Hz high-pass to remove rumble/hum
3. Level
   +1.5 dB gain with a gentle look-ahead peak limiter (max 3.1 dB on a few peaks).
   Result: -19 LUFS, true peak -1.5 dBFS. No compression, EQ, pitch or timing changes.
   No music, no added sound.
- The user (Rajesh) has no spoken audio in this section; his question appears on screen
  as text, so no user voice needed preserving.

AUDIO REVISION — sales_coach_ask_in_context_final_audio_clean.mp4
=====================================================
Why: the first audio pass still had room noise, a muddy tone, and audible doubling
(the delayed copy of the AI voice) in places.

Video: the H.264 stream is copied bit-for-bit from sales_coach_ask_in_context_final.mp4
(per-frame checksums match on all 631 frames). Crop, resolution, UI, timing and the
screen 19 start are unchanged.

Audio was rebuilt from the ORIGINAL source recording (not the previous mix):
1. Source audio cut at the screen-19 frame (2.569 s), the same cut as the video.
2. Neural speech enhancement: DeepFilterNet3, run locally (no cloud and no voice
   generation). It removes the room hiss/hum and most of the audible doubling.
   Its 30 ms processing delay is compensated exactly.
   Suppression is capped at 30 dB so pauses fall to a quiet floor instead of
   hard-gating to silence between syllables, which sounds choppy.
3. Late-echo suppression: removes what's left of the ~110 ms delayed copy of the voice
   (laptop speaker picked up by the mic), only where that copy is present.
4. Tone restoration: the denoiser had pulled the voice's 315–2500 Hz clarity band
   down 6–9 dB, which made it muddy. A linear-phase EQ, measured against the source
   voice's own loudest moments, restores the original tonal balance (now within about
   ±1–2.5 dB of the source in every band). Plus a 60 Hz high-pass.
5. Level: -17.1 LUFS, peak -2.3 dBFS. The limiter touches <5% of voice samples by
   at most 2.7 dB. The mic hiss before the AI starts speaking is faded out inside
   the source's own 70 ms dropout (3.0–3.1 s), so the cut is inaudible.

Measured results (source -> revised):
- Doubling: envelope repetition at ~110 ms (z-score; ~0 means no copy)
    sentence 3 ("I'd also ask about…"): 3.8 -> -1.3 ; all zones now <= 0.7
- Noise floor between sentences: -29.2 dB -> -54.8 dB; before the voice: silent
- Sync: within 1 sample (0.02 ms) of the source in all three sentences
- Words: unchanged. The same three original sentences at the same timestamps; speech
  recognition now hears "AWS spend", "validated" and "workload" more clearly than in the source.
- This is the original Sales Coach voice. No TTS, no cloning, no re-reading of screen text.

Note: these checks are objective measurements. I could not literally listen on headphones.

AUDIO REVISION 2 — sales_coach_ask_in_context_final_audio_clean_v2.mp4
=====================================================
Scope: only two short transition points were edited. All edits are gain-only
(attenuation and fades) applied to the audio_clean mix; no samples change outside
the windows below (verified: 0 samples). The video stream is copied bit-for-bit.

1. AI voice start (3.18–3.26 s)
   Cause: the source recording has a ~200 ms digital dropout where the browser started
   playback. Audio resumed with a hard step at 3.1795 s (click), followed by a faint
   noise bed before the first word.
   Fix: silence until 3.245 s, then a 12 ms raised-cosine fade-in ending at the first
   glottal pulse of "Based" (3.257 s). The voice is not delayed and its level is
   unchanged from 3.26 s on.

2. Subtitles-off transition (screen switch at 15.983 s)
   a) 15.988–16.038 s: a 22 ms buzzy packet that was not in the source. The denoiser
      rendered the quiet pause right after the switch as a burst. Fix: -20 dB with
      4 ms ramps (the pause is now -67 dB).
   b) 16.232–16.26 s: a 30 ms periodic burst at ~194 Hz (about twice the voice's pitch),
      consistent with the trackpad's haptic click being picked up by the mic. The denoiser
      kept its head and removed the rest, leaving a pop about 10 dB above the voice.
      Fix: levelled to the surrounding voice level (2.5 ms envelope, 3 ms smoothing,
      inside 16.222–16.292 s only).

Re-verified on the exported MP4: no isolated pops or hard steps remain (the
spike/step scan flags only natural syllables, which have the same shape as in
the source). Echo z-scores stay <= 0.9 in all zones. Noise floor between
sentences -54.8 dB. Sync within 1 sample of the source. Words unchanged.
-17.3 LUFS, peak -2.3 dBFS.
